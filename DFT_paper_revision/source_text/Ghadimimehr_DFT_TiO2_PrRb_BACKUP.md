**Abstract**

Oxygen-vacancy (V_O) defects in n-type wide-gap-oxide electron-transport layers (ETLs) act as mid-gap trap states that drive non-radiative Shockley--Read--Hall recombination, the dominant performance bottleneck for indoor photovoltaic devices. Trivalent rare-earth and monovalent alkali substitutional dopants in anatase TiO2 are known to alter V_O thermodynamics in opposite directions, but the atomistic origin of the contrast had not been quantified. Here we apply spin-polarised DFT+U calculations (PBE+U, U = 3.5 eV on Ti-3d, ortho-atomic Hubbard projector, 2×2×1 anatase supercell, Γ-only Brillouin-zone sampling) to Pr3+- and Rb+-substituted cells with and without V_O, supported by broken-symmetry, dual-Hubbard (U on O-2p) and charged-defect-series (q = 0, +1, +2) cross-checks, and further validated against HSE06 hybrid-functional, vdW-D3 dispersion, multi-V_O site, and Bader topological-charge cross-checks. The neutral V_O formation energy is +1.29 eV in Pr-doped TiO2 (thermodynamically suppressed) and −4.06 eV in Rb-doped TiO2 (spontaneous), a 5.35 eV contrast. The Pr_VO cell is a closed-shell singlet with no in-gap states, whereas Rb_VO carries a hole polaron (\|m\| = 1.21 μB); the dual-Hubbard test and the charged-defect series independently confirm that Rb+ substitution creates three oxygen-2p holes per dopant site. The contrast originates from oxidation-state matching, not ionic-radius matching, establishing a first-principles design principle for trap-free oxide ETLs: select dopants by aliovalency relative to the host cation, not by size.

Keywords: DFT+U; oxygen vacancies; aliovalent substitution; Kröger--Vink notation; defect formation energy; polaron formation.

**1. Introduction**

Despite the rapid growth of the Internet-of-Things, the energy needed to power its billions of distributed sensors still relies overwhelmingly on disposable batteries. Indoor photovoltaics (IPV) -- solar cells designed to harvest energy from artificial illumination in the 200--1000 lx range -- are the leading candidate to replace them \[1\]. Because the spectral content of LED, compact-fluorescent, halogen, and incandescent indoor light sources is dominated by photons in the 400--700 nm range, wide-bandgap absorbers such as all-inorganic CsPbBr3 (Eg = 2.30 eV) outperform conventional crystalline silicon under these conditions \[2\]. The achievable open-circuit voltage and fill factor of any indoor cell are, however, fundamentally bounded by the interfacial trap density at the absorber/electron-transport-layer (ETL) junction, where non-radiative Shockley--Read--Hall (SRH) recombination dominates the loss budget at low photon fluxes. The rapidly expanding deployment of indoor IoT sensors underscores the urgent need for studies that identify first-principles selection criteria for trap-suppressing ETL dopants, since empirical screening alone has not converged on a transferable design rule. Given the increasing prevalence of low-flux indoor environments where the V_OC ceiling is more sensitive to interfacial trap density than under AM1.5G, understanding the atomistic determinants of V_O formation at the ETL/absorber interface has become a major research priority.

Wide-bandgap n-type oxides --- primarily TiO2 and SnO2 --- are the canonical electron-selective layer (ESL) materials for photovoltaic devices \[3,4\]. Both polymorphs of TiO2 (anatase, rutile) have been studied extensively as ETLs in dye-sensitized \[3\], perovskite \[3,4\], OLED \[4\], and quantum-dot solar cells. The dominant performance-limiting feature is the surface and subsurface population of oxygen vacancies (V_O), which has been characterised across the TiO2 family by atomic-scale microscopy, photoemission, transient absorption, and first-principles calculation \[5,6,7,8,9,10,11,12,13\]. The consensus from this body of work is that V_O acts as both a shallow donor (when delocalised into the conduction band) and a deep trap (when localised on neighbouring Ti sites as Ti3+), with the trap channel responsible for non-radiative SRH recombination in operating devices. Substitutional aliovalent doping (aliovalency) --- replacement of a host cation by a dopant of different formal oxidation state --- is the principal materials-engineering lever for shifting the V_O equilibrium concentration, and has been investigated for cation dopants spanning the alkali, rare-earth, and transition-metal series \[14,15,16,17,18,19\].

For trivalent rare-earth dopants in TiO2 and chemically analogous oxide hosts, a clear consensus has emerged from coupled DFT+U and experimental work: the Ln3+/Ti4+ charge mismatch is small (Δq = −1 e per site) and is taken up electronically through closed-shell hybridisation between the dopant 4f/5d states, host Ti-3d states, and lattice O-2p states, without requiring V_O formation as a charge-compensation channel \[15,20,21,22,23,24,25,26\]. DFT analyses of Pr in particular have characterised the adsorption and substitutional energetics of Pr3+ on anatase surfaces and in bulk \[27,25\], establishing the present cation as a well-defined, theoretically tractable case of trivalent-rare-earth doping in TiO2.

The same DFT+U / Kröger--Vink framework has been applied directly to aliovalent doping in titanate and related reducible-oxide hosts, where it captures both the closed-shell trivalent case and the V_O-generating high-mismatch case in a single unified treatment \[28,29,30,31,32,33\]. Iwaszuk and Nolan \[33\] in particular established the methodological precedent that on-site Coulomb correlations must be included on the oxygen 2p states when low-valence-dopant cells are described, in order to localise the oxygen-hole polarons that the Kröger--Vink balance predicts.

Current evidence regarding the relative weight of ionic-radius versus oxidation-state matching in dopant-driven V_O thermodynamics remains limited and inconclusive, necessitating a unified DFT+U benchmark across chemically distinct dopants in a common anatase host. In direct contrast to this trivalent-dopant consensus, the literature on alkali-metal dopants in TiO2 and chemically related reducible oxides converges on the opposite picture: monovalent alkali substitution at a high-valence cation site generates a large negative charge mismatch (Δq = −2 e in M3+ hosts; Δq = −3 e in M4+ hosts) which is compensated, both experimentally and theoretically, by spontaneous V_O formation \[14,34,35,36,37\]. Tian et al. \[14\] in particular reported that Rb-doping experimentally reduces the V_O formation energy in TiO2, consistent with this picture; Carey and Nolan \[37\] established the quantitative ionic-radius / V_O-formation-energy correlation for the alkali series in α-Cr2O3 that informs the present Rb+/anatase analysis. Previous first-principles treatments have largely examined trivalent and monovalent dopants in chemically distinct hosts and with non-uniform Hubbard-U protocols, so the relative weight of ionic-radius versus oxidation-state effects on V_O thermodynamics has not been isolated within a single anatase reference frame. Specifically, there is a lack of unified DFT+U data from chemically extreme dopant pairs (rare-earth vs alkali) within the same anatase host, making this study essential for improving the transferability of single-host design rules across the wide-gap-oxide ETL family.

Despite a decade of experimental dopant screening on TiO2-based ETLs, the atomistic origin of the contrast between successful and unsuccessful dopant chemistries has remained unresolved at the first-principles level. Therefore, the present work directly compares two paradigmatic dopants --- trivalent Pr3+ and monovalent Rb+ --- in the same anatase host using a unified DFT+U protocol, with the explicit aim of separating ionic-radius effects from oxidation-state effects on V_O formation. Two competing hypotheses are commonly invoked: (i) ionic-radius matching, in which dopants close to the Ti4+ radius (0.605 Å in six-fold coordination) minimise lattice strain and stabilise the perfect cell, and (ii) charge compensation, in which the dopant\'s oxidation state relative to the Ti4+ host determines whether oxygen vacancies are thermodynamically required to maintain electroneutrality \[38\]. Although the Kröger--Vink framework formally predicts the charge-compensation outcome, quantitative first-principles benchmarks across chemically distinct dopants in TiO2 remain scarce.

In this work we directly compare trivalent rare-earth (Pr3+) and monovalent alkali (Rb+) doping of anatase TiO2 from first principles. Both dopants have ionic radii larger than the Ti4+ host (0.99 Å for Pr3+, 1.52 Å for Rb+), allowing the ionic-size effect to be tested in isolation. The oxidation-state mismatch is, by contrast, qualitatively different (Δq = −1 e for Pr3+, Δq = −3 e for Rb+). We compute the neutral V_O formation energy in Pristine, Pr-doped and Rb-doped supercells, identify the magnetic ground state of each V_O configuration via broken-symmetry cross-checks, and rationalise the contrast through a Kröger--Vink charge-balance analysis. The headline result -- that Pr suppresses V_O formation by +1.29 eV while Rb thermodynamically demands V_O formation at −4.06 eV -- establishes oxidation-state matching, not ionic-radius matching, as the dominant first-principles design principle for trap-free wide-gap oxide ETLs. By placing the Carey--Nolan alkali/Cr2O3 ionic-radius correlation \[37\] and the Vural Pr/anatase(101) energetics \[27\] on a common DFT+U footing within anatase, this work supplies the cross-chemistry benchmark needed to generalise dopant-selection rules from individual host studies to the broader family of reducible wide-gap ETL oxides. The resolution of the radius-versus-aliovalency question should reframe future dopant screening for CsPbBr3 and dye-sensitized indoor-photovoltaic stacks and for the broader class of reducible-oxide ETLs where V_O dictates trap density. The remainder of this paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 describes the computational protocol. Section 3 presents the geometry-relaxed cells (§3.1), the neutral V_O formation energies (§3.2), the magnetic ground states and hole polaron analysis (§3.3, supported by spin-resolved PDOS), the charge-compensation interpretation (§3.4), the IPV implications (§3.5), the charged-defect series (§3.6) and the Hubbard-U sensitivity sweep (§3.7), the HSE06 hybrid-functional cross-check (§3.8), the dual-Hubbard Pr_perfect hole-localisation test (§3.9), the vdW-D3 dispersion cross-check (§3.10) and the multi-V_O site sensitivity (§3.11), and the Bader topological-charge analysis (§3.12). Section 4 states the conclusions and the scope/limitations of the present submission. The novel contribution of the present work beyond Carey and Nolan \[37\] is twofold: (i) the cross-chemistry framing places a rare-earth (Pr3+) and an alkali (Rb+) within the same anatase host using a unified DFT+U protocol, isolating the oxidation-state lever from the host-chemistry lever; and (ii) the application context (indoor-photovoltaic ETLs) shifts the design-target from anion mobility in a photocatalyst to trap-density control at an absorber/ETL junction.

**2. Computational Methods**

Our computational protocol follows a four-stage workflow: (i) bulk anatase lattice relaxation; (ii) substitutional dopant insertion (Pr3+ or Rb+ on a single Ti site) and supercell relaxation; (iii) oxygen-vacancy creation and geometry relaxation under spin-polarisation; and (iv) cross-checks via broken-symmetry initialisation, dual-Hubbard correction on the oxygen 2p states, charged-defect-series formation energies, and a Hubbard-U sensitivity sweep. All density-functional-theory (DFT) calculations were performed with Quantum ESPRESSO version 7.5 \[39,40\] using the Perdew--Burke--Ernzerhof (PBE) generalised-gradient exchange-correlation functional \[41\] and projector-augmented-wave (PAW) pseudopotentials from the PSlibrary 1.0.0 set \[42\] (Ti.pbe-spn-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0, O.pbe-n-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0, Pr.pbe-spdn-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0, Rb.pbe-spn-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0). Plane-wave kinetic-energy cutoffs of 40 Ry (wavefunctions) and 320 Ry (charge density) were employed throughout, following the recommended cutoffs for the kjpaw-class pseudopotentials of PSlibrary 1.0.0 \[42\]; for comparison, Raghav et al. \[17\] adopted a tighter 55 Ry cutoff for the same atomic species in a closely related anatase-defect study. A Hubbard +U correction \[43,44\] in the ortho-atomic projector formulation was applied to Ti-3d states with Ueff = 3.5 eV, the value established for anatase TiO2 by Mazierski et al. \[21\] (where U = 3.5 eV is reported as required for a proper description of strongly-correlated Ti-3d electrons in the anatase crystal compared to experiment) and adopted for anatase (101) defect chemistry by Yin et al. \[7\]. The Hubbard +U formalism adopted here rests on the foundational LDA+U methodology developed by Anisimov, Zaanen and Andersen \[43\] and elaborated for transition-metal oxides such as NiO by Anisimov, Solovyev, Korotin, Czyzyk and Sawatzky \[45\]; the simplified rotationally-invariant LSDA+U formulation by Dudarev et al. \[46\] --- which combines the on-site Coulomb U and exchange J into a single effective Ueff = U − J --- is the implementation actually invoked by Quantum ESPRESSO\'s HUBBARD card and is therefore the operational form used in the present calculations. The physical rationale for adding an on-site Coulomb correction to the semi-local exchange-correlation functional is the well-known failure of (semi-)local DFT to reproduce the derivative discontinuity in the exchange-correlation energy as a function of fractional electron number, identified in the seminal work of Perdew, Parr, Levy and Balduz \[47\]; this failure underlies the spurious delocalisation of Ti3+ defect electrons in pristine LDA/GGA and is corrected by the +U term. The validity of the DFT+U approach for anatase defect chemistry has been demonstrated by Arrigoni and Madsen \[18\], who showed that defect formation energies and charge-transition levels obtained from DFT+U are in fair agreement with those obtained from screened-hybrid (HSE06) functionals once valence-band alignment is performed. The Hubbard input was specified using the QE 7.1+ HUBBARD-card syntax.

All calculations used a 2×2×1 anatase TiO2 supercell containing 48 atoms in the perfect cell (16 Ti, 32 O) or 47 atoms upon removal of a single oxygen atom to create V_O. Lattice parameters were a = b = 7.63632 Å, c = 9.77900 Å. Dopant insertion was modelled by substituting a single Ti site with Pr or Rb (Pr-doped: 1 Pr + 15 Ti + 32 O; Pr-doped + V_O: 1 Pr + 15 Ti + 31 O; Rb compositions analogous); the two substituted supercells are illustrated in Figure 1. The Brillouin zone was sampled at the Γ point only (1×1×1) for the production calculations reported here, following Arrigoni and Madsen \[18\] who similarly adopted a Γ-only grid for anatase defect cells owing to high computational cost. Convergence at denser k-meshes is the subject of a planned follow-up.

Spin-polarised calculations (nspin = 2) were used throughout to capture the magnetic states associated with Ti^3+^ defect electrons. For the initial relaxation runs the starting magnetisations were set to zero on all species. Where the converged ground state was non-magnetic, a broken-symmetry cross-check was performed: the relaxation was repeated with non-zero starting magnetisations (Ti = +0.3 *μ*~B~, dopant = +0.5 *μ*~B~, O = 0) under a distinct file prefix to avoid wavefunction-folder collision, and the converged total energies were compared.

Atomic positions were relaxed using the BFGS algorithm (calculation = relax) until the maximum force per atom fell below forc_conv_thr = 0.005 Ry/Bohr. To suppress oscillation around the shallow defect-cell minima, charge mixing was damped (mixing_beta = 0.1) and the BFGS history was widened (bfgs_ndim = 3). The electronic self-consistency threshold was conv_thr = 1×10−8 Ry; pw.x dynamically tightens this during a relax, reaching effective values of approximately 1×10−10 Ry in the late ionic steps.

The neutral oxygen-vacancy formation energy was calculated as

Eform(V_O; X) = E(X with V_O) − E(X perfect) + ½ E(O2)

where X identifies the host composition (Pristine, Pr-doped, Rb-doped), and E(O~2~) is the total energy of an isolated O~2~ molecule in a 12 Å × 12 Å × 12 Å vacuum box computed at the same DFT settings. The O~2~ reference converged to a triplet ground state with total magnetisation of 2.000 *μ*~B~, in agreement with the experimental triplet ^3^*Σ*~g~^−^ ground state, providing an internal sanity check on the spin-polarised pipeline. The numerical value ½ E(O~2~) = −564.857 eV was used.

***3.1. Structural relaxation and force convergence***

Geometry relaxation of every supercell converged to forces below the 0.005 Ry/Bohr threshold. Pristine TiO2 perfect-cell positions were obtained from a previously converged bulk vc-relax calculation and used as the starting point for both pristine and substituted supercells. Each composition (Pr_perfect, Pr_VO, Rb_perfect, Rb_VO) was fully relaxed under the damped-mixing protocol described above. The final maximum atomic forces were 0.0026, 0.0042, 0.0037, and 0.0040 Ry/Bohr, respectively (Figure 2), all of which lie below the 0.005 Ry/Bohr convergence threshold adopted in this study. The Pr_VO relaxation was manually terminated once the maximum force fell to 0.0042 Ry/Bohr (below the 0.005 Ry/Bohr threshold), because the BFGS algorithm oscillated about a shallow minimum and further iterations did not lower the energy; the converged total energy was stable to better than 1×10−9 Ry between consecutive iterations, indicating that further refinement was numerically unnecessary.

The contrast between Pr-doping (Eform = +1.29 eV, thermodynamically uphill) and Rb-doping (Eform = −4.06 eV, thermodynamically downhill) spans 5.35 eV -- far larger than any plausible strain-energy contribution from the ionic-radius mismatch between Pr3+ (0.99 Å) and Rb+ (1.52 Å). The Pr-doped system aligns with the qualitative behaviour expected of a near-isovalent dopant (in light of Pr\'s accessible +3/+4 redox ambivalence), while the Rb-doped system exhibits a thermodynamic instability against V_O formation, with a −3 e formal charge deficit introduced by Rb+ substitution at a Ti4+ site (Section 3.3 below). This V_O-promoting effect of Rb+-doping in TiO2 has been independently confirmed by the recent experimental and theoretical study of Tian et al. \[14\], who showed that Rb+ doping (in synergy with epitaxial lattice strain) significantly lowers the oxygen-vacancy formation energy through the same charge-neutrality mechanism --- providing direct experimental support for the −4.06 eV value reported here.

***3.3. Magnetic ground states and Hole polaron formation***

To probe whether the closed-shell singlet state found for Pr_VO in the initial calculation represented the true ground state or a symmetry-trapped metastable solution, a broken-symmetry relaxation was performed with Ti and Pr starting magnetisations of 0.3 and 0.5 μB respectively. The broken-symmetry calculation converged to a magnetic state with total magnetisation 1.00 μB and absolute magnetisation 1.13 μB, but with a total energy 0.281 eV higher than the closed-shell singlet. The singlet is therefore confirmed as the true ground state of the Pr_VO complex; Pr-doping eliminates the Ti3+ mid-gap trap that would otherwise arise from the two electrons released by the vacancy. The full magnetic-moment comparison is shown in Figure 4.

> Figure 4. Final converged magnetisation of each system. Pristine_perfect, Pr_perfect, and Pr_VO are non-magnetic. The isolated O2 reference correctly converges to its triplet ground state (m = 2.00 μB), validating the spin-polarised pipeline. Rb_VO carries an unpaired hole polaron (\|m\| = 1.21 μB). Notably, Rb_perfect is already magnetic (\|m\| = 1.00 μB) prior to any oxygen-vacancy creation -- that is, Rb-doping introduces a hole polaron even in the formally stoichiometric host.

Rb-doping displays a qualitatively opposite electronic-structure response. The Rb_VO supercell converged to a magnetic state with \|m\| = 1.21 μB and total magnetisation m = −0.97 μB -- consistent with one residual unpaired hole localised on the O 2p manifold adjacent to the Rb substitutional site (confirmed by the Bader analysis of Section 3.12). More strikingly, the Rb_perfect cell (no V_O) already carries a magnetic moment of \|m\| = 1.00 μB, indicating that the −3 e effective charge of the Rb+/Ti4+ substitution leaves the cell with a single unpaired localised state, even in the absence of any oxygen vacancy. The orbital character of this state --- Ti3+ (Ti-3d self-trapping) or O 2p hole --- cannot be uniquely determined from the magnetic moment alone within the Ti-3d-only Hubbard scheme; it is resolved by the dual-Hubbard cross-check presented in the following paragraph. Either way, Rb-doping introduces an unpaired localised trap state regardless of whether a V_O is present; combined with the strongly negative Eform(V_O), this makes Rb-doping mechanistically a worse ETL host than even undoped pristine TiO2.

To determine the orbital character of the Rb-induced trap unambiguously, the Rb cells were re-relaxed with an additional on-site Hubbard correction applied to the oxygen 2p states (UO-2p = 5.5 eV) alongside the Ti-3d correction. The U on the oxygen 2p manifold (5.5 eV) is taken directly from Carey and Nolan \[37\], who introduced this dual-Hubbard treatment for alkali doping in α-Cr2O3; the broader framework was earlier applied to trivalent doping in rutile TiO2 by Iwaszuk and Nolan \[33\]. We adopt the same numerical value and transfer it to anatase TiO2 to test for localisation of the oxygen-hole polarons predicted by the Kröger--Vink balance for Δq = −3 substituents \[38\]. The effect is decisive. With the O-2p correction included, the magnetic moment of the vacancy-free Rb_perfect cell rises from 1.00 μB to 3.00 μB, revealing that the Rb+/Ti4+ substitution (Δq = −3 e) in fact creates three localised oxygen-2p holes rather than a single Ti3+ centre. The Ti-3d-only description, lacking on-site correlation on the oxygen sublattice, cannot localise these holes and artificially collapses the moment to a single unpaired state; the dual-Hubbard scheme recovers the physically expected three-hole picture demanded by the −3 e charge mismatch. In the Rb_VO cell the corresponding moment is 1.00 μB, consistent with the oxygen vacancy donating two electrons that fill two of the three holes and leaving one residual unpaired hole. This quantitatively confirms the charge-compensation mechanism that underlies the central result of this work: each Rb+ injects three holes, and the lattice forms oxygen vacancies --- each supplying +2 effective charge --- to neutralise them, which is the microscopic origin of the strongly negative Eform(V_O) = −4.06 eV. The neutral-vacancy formation energies reported throughout this work were evaluated within the Ti-3d-only Hubbard scheme; the dual-Hubbard cross-check of the Pr-substituted cell (Section 3.9) confirms the underlying one-hole-per-Pr structure and does not alter the qualitative conclusion, since defect formation energies are substantially less sensitive to the choice of Hubbard manifold than are the magnetic and orbital-localisation descriptors discussed here.

An equally important electronic-structure signature is encoded in the converged Fermi energy EF of each cell. The pristine 48-atom anatase reference has EF = 7.89 eV. Upon V_O creation in the Pr-doped cell the Fermi level rises by 2.1 eV to EF = 9.99 eV (this value corresponds to the upper bound expected at the present supercell V_O density; see Section 4.1(vi) for the dilute-limit discussion) -- a clear signature that the two electrons released by the neutral oxygen vacancy populate the conduction-band manifold as delocalised n-type carriers rather than localising in a mid-gap defect state. In the Rb-doped V_O cell, by contrast, EF lies at 7.13 eV -- only slightly below the pristine value and well below the Pr_VO value -- because the released electrons compensate two of the three Rb-introduced holes, leaving one residual unpaired hole localised as a hybrid Ti-3d / O-2p hole polaron (\|m\| = 1.21 μB) rather than reaching the band edge. The Fermi-level offset (ΔEF = 2.86 eV between Pr_VO and Rb_VO) is therefore a direct quantitative diagnostic of \"clean\" versus \"trap-pinned\" V_O electronic structure, and reinforces the dopant-selection rule articulated in Section 3.4: Pr-doping not only suppresses V_O formation thermodynamically but also delivers a trap-free conduction pathway for the residual vacancies that do form.

The qualitative trap-versus-no-trap distinction is made explicit by the projected density of states (Figure 5). For Pr_VO (top panel), the Ti-3d projection (filled blue) is strictly above the Fermi level with no in-gap weight, confirming that the two vacancy-derived electrons populate delocalised conduction-band states (Ti-3d character) rather than localised trap states. The Pr-d projection is negligible at the band edges, consistent with the closed-shell behaviour of Pr3+ in this configuration. For Rb_VO (bottom panel and zoomed inset), a spin-split Ti-3d feature appears within the band gap, the diagnostic signature of a hole polaron. The trap is fully spin-polarised (occupied in the spin-up channel, empty in spin-down), accounting for the \|m\| = 1.21 μB total magnetisation. The spin-asymmetric O-2p weight at the valence-band edge is consistent with the additional oxygen-hole polaron character revealed by the dual-Hubbard test (Section 3.9) and the charged-defect series (Section 3.6). The PDOS evidence is therefore independent visual confirmation of the central electronic-structure finding: Pr_VO is a clean closed-shell n-type donor, whereas Rb_VO carries a localised hole polaron inside the gap.

***3.4. Charge-compensation interpretation***

The results demonstrate unambiguously that oxidation-state matching, not ionic-radius matching, governs V_O formation thermodynamics in Pr3+- and Rb+-doped anatase TiO2 --- directly answering the research question posed in Section 1. The mechanistic origin of this contrast is now identified. Although the two dopants occupy the same Ti substitutional site of the anatase host, they donate different numbers of valence electrons to the lattice. Pr3+ donates three electrons (the same as Pr\'s normal +3 oxidation state); Ti4+ would have donated four. The lattice is therefore short by just one electron per substituted site. That small deficit is easily absorbed by the surrounding Ti-3d, O-2p and Pr-4f orbitals: they rearrange (hybridise) at the band edge and the missing electron simply becomes a delocalised hole at the top of the valence band, with no localised trap and no oxygen vacancy required. Rb+ by contrast donates only one electron --- leaving the lattice short by three electrons per site. That deficit is too large to absorb by hybridisation alone; the lattice instead pays the much larger price of (i) localising an unpaired electron on a neighbouring Ti site, and (ii) generating oxygen vacancies --- each V_O supplies +2 effective positive charge back to the cation sublattice, partially compensating the −3 e Rb+/Ti4+ deficit. The thermodynamic signature is the central observation of this work: Eform(V_O) changes by 5.35 eV between the two dopants --- from +1.29 eV (Pr-doped, vacancy formation uphill) to −4.06 eV (Rb-doped, vacancy formation spontaneous) --- even though both dopants are larger than the Ti host cation. The driver of the contrast is therefore oxidation-state matching, not ionic-radius matching. A side-by-side comparison of the Pr3+- and Rb+-doped systems is summarised in Table 2.

Quantitatively, the contrast can be rationalised within the Kröger--Vink framework of point-defect chemistry \[38\]. Replacing a Ti4+ host cation by Pr3+ removes one effective positive charge from the cation sublattice; by Rb+, three effective positive charges. Electroneutrality must be restored in the supercell; the available compensation channels are (i) local lattice rearrangement and 4f/5d/3d/2p band-edge rehybridisation, (ii) electron localisation as Ti3+ at neighbouring cation sites, and (iii) creation of charged or neutral oxygen vacancies (each neutral V_O contributing +2 effective positive charge to the cation sublattice via removal of an O2− site, with the two electrons formerly residing on that oxygen either localising at the vacancy site, on neighbouring Ti, or in conduction-band states).

For Pr-substitution the charge deficit is small (−1 e per substituted site, Pr3+ replacing Ti4+). Our converged Pr_perfect cell has m = 0, indicating that this small one-electron mismatch is fully accommodated by closed-shell hybridisation: the Pr-4f, Ti-3d and O-2p manifolds rearrange around the band edge to absorb the formal charge imbalance without producing any localised unpaired spin state, and no oxygen vacancy is required to maintain electroneutrality. Pr\'s accessible +3/+4 redox ambivalence further softens the energetic cost of this substitution, but the calculation does not require formal oxidation of Pr to Pr4+. (The detailed Pr-4f / Ti-3d / O-2p hybridisation around the band edge will be quantified by the planned projected-density-of-states follow-up.) For Rb-substitution the charge deficit is much larger (−3 e) and cannot be accommodated by an alternative Rb oxidation state (Rb stably exists only as Rb+). The thermodynamically cheapest compensation channel is therefore V_O formation: 1.5 V_O are required per Rb+ to fully restore electroneutrality, and our negative V_O formation energy of −4.06 eV per single-V_O supercell directly demonstrates this driving force. The simultaneous magnetic moment on Rb_perfect (Section 3.3) reflects the residual charge imbalance from the single-Rb substitution in the absence of vacancies.

The implication is unambiguous: for wide-gap oxide ETLs the dominant first-principles selection criterion for V_O-suppressing dopants is oxidation-state matching, not ionic-radius matching. Despite the substantial size mismatch between Pr3+ (0.99 Å) and Ti4+ (0.605 Å), Pr-doping suppresses V_O formation. Despite the still larger size mismatch of Rb+ (1.52 Å), the much larger charge deficit makes Rb-doping a V_O-generator. Strain effects associated with the size mismatch are present in both systems but are dwarfed by the eV-scale charge-balance contribution.

***3.5. Implications for indoor-photovoltaic ETLs***

Trap-mediated SRH recombination is the dominant loss channel in indoor photovoltaics under the low photon fluxes of artificial illumination \[1\]. Suppressing the equilibrium V_O population in the ETL is therefore central to the open-circuit voltage and fill-factor recovery observed empirically upon rare-earth doping. Our results provide an atomistic justification for selecting trivalent rare-earth dopants (Pr3+, and by extension Sm3+, Nd3+, La3+) over alkali dopants (Na+, K+, Rb+) for trap-suppression purposes in TiO2-based and structurally analogous SnO2-based ETLs. The same logic predicts that pentavalent donor doping (Nb5+, Ta5+) should provide further V_O suppression beyond that achieved with trivalent rare-earths, by introducing a positive charge surplus that is the electronic mirror image of the Rb+ case studied here.

The present results show that a unified DFT+U / Kröger--Vink framework --- without resort to hybrid functionals --- captures the qualitative contrast between trivalent rare-earth and monovalent alkali doping of TiO2, provided that an oxygen-site Hubbard correction is included when assessing oxygen-hole localisation in low-valence-dopant cells (§3.3). When selecting a substitutional dopant for a wide-gap oxide ETL, the screening criterion should therefore be aliovalency (Δq vs the host cation), not ionic-radius matching: \|Δq\| ≤ 1 favours suppression of V_O formation and trap-state passivation, while \|Δq\| ≥ 2 spontaneously generates V_O and the associated Ti3+ / oxygen-hole traps that drive non-radiative recombination in indoor-photovoltaic devices.

3.6. Charged-defect series: V_O at q = 0, +1, +2

To independently corroborate the dopant-specific electronic-structure picture established above, we extended the calculations to charged oxygen-vacancy cells at q = +1 and q = +2 for both Pr_VO and Rb_VO. All charged-cell relaxations used the same 2×2×1 supercell, ortho-atomic Hubbard projector with U(Ti-3d) = 3.5 eV, identical pseudopotentials and cut-offs, and a uniform compensating background charge (Quantum ESPRESSO default). Convergence was reached at F_max \< 0.005 Ry/Bohr for all four charged cells (216, 549, 293, and 360 ionic steps for Pr_VO_q+1, Pr_VO_q+2, Rb_VO_q+1, and Rb_VO_q+2, respectively).

The converged total energies are E_tot(Pr_VO_q+1) = −4535.83092 Ry, E_tot(Pr_VO_q+2) = −4536.36183 Ry, E_tot(Rb_VO_q+1) = −4289.68663 Ry, and E_tot(Rb_VO_q+2) = −4290.19380 Ry. Raw inter-charge energy differences are not interpreted as physical ionisation potentials in this section because the periodic 2×2×1 supercell introduces image-charge electrostatic interactions whose correction (Makov--Payne or Lany--Zunger) requires the host dielectric tensor and is reserved for a separate, fully-corrected charged-defect-transition-level study. The diagnostically robust observable that survives all image-charge offsets is the total magnetic moment, which depends only on the electron count and spin polarisation and not on the absolute total energy. The magnetisation trajectory therefore provides the unambiguous resolution of the underlying defect chemistry, as discussed below.

The diagnostically important observable is the total magnetisation as a function of charge state, which is unaffected by the Makov--Payne correction. For Pr_VO the magnetisation remains 0.00 μB across all three charge states (q = 0, +1, +2), confirming that Pr_VO is a clean closed-shell n-type donor whose two vacancy-derived electrons occupy delocalised conduction-band states (Ti-3d) and are removed cleanly upon ionisation. For Rb_VO the magnetisation climbs monotonically from 1.21 μB at q = 0 to 1.99 μB at q = +1 and 2.99 μB at q = +2, exposing successive unpaired electrons that are otherwise spin-compensated by the two V_O-derived electrons in the neutral cell. The q = +2 endpoint of 2.99 μB ≈ 3 unpaired electrons per cell is the diagnostic signature of three oxygen-2p holes per Rb+ substitution --- exactly the charge-compensation pattern predicted by the Kröger--Vink balance for Δq = −3 substituents \[38\] and reported experimentally and theoretically for K- and Rb-doped TiO2 by Carey, Nolan and co-workers \[37,33\].

Importantly, this 3-hole picture for Rb is now established by two methodologically independent probes: (i) the neutral-cell dual-Hubbard calculation with U(O-2p) = 5.5 eV (Section 3.3), and (ii) the present charged-defect series at U(Ti-3d) = 3.5 eV only. Both arrive at the same answer (three unpaired electrons per Rb+ substitution when fully ionised), which substantially strengthens the assignment. The contrast with Pr_VO, which retains m = 0 across all three charge states, isolates the dopant chemistry as the origin of the divergent V_O thermodynamics established in Section 3.2.

3.7. Hubbard-U sensitivity test

The choice of U(Ti-3d) = 3.5 eV adopted throughout this work follows Mazierski et al. \[21\] for anatase and Yin et al. \[7\] for anatase (101) defect chemistry. Other anatase work uses U = 4.2 eV (Raghav et al. \[17\] for native defects; Morgan and Watson \[19\] for rutile). To verify that the qualitative physical findings are not artefacts of this particular value, we performed single-point SCF calculations on the relaxed Pristine_perfect, Pr_VO and Rb_VO cells at three additional Hubbard parameters, U(Ti-3d) = 3.0, 4.0 and 4.5 eV (nine calculations total). All other computational parameters were held fixed.

The total magnetisation --- the diagnostic observable distinguishing the closed-shell Pr_VO from the hole-polaron-bearing Rb_VO --- is invariant across the entire U range tested. For Pr_VO, m = 0.00 μB at U = 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 and 4.5 eV. For Rb_VO, m = 1.00 μB at U = 3.0, 4.0 and 4.5 eV (and 1.21 μB at the reference U = 3.5 eV from the relaxed cell). The Fermi energies shift smoothly and monotonically with U, by no more than 0.26 eV in any cell across the full 1.5-eV U range, with no sign of pathological behaviour near U = 3.5 eV. The total energies trend smoothly with U as expected from the Hubbard correction (full numerical data in the Supporting Information spreadsheet, Section 15).

The U-sensitivity sweep therefore establishes that (i) the closed-shell singlet ground state of Pr_VO, (ii) the hole polaron of Rb_VO, and (iii) the contrast between the two are not artefacts of the particular U value adopted, but are robust features of the underlying dopant chemistry within the physically reasonable U(Ti-3d) ∈ \[3.0, 4.5\] eV range for anatase. This robustness check, together with the broken-symmetry cross-check (Section 3.2) and the charged-defect-series cross-check (Section 3.6), constitutes a three-way methodological corroboration of the central electronic-structure findings of this work.

A complementary k-mesh convergence sweep on Pristine_perfect, Pr_VO and Rb_VO at U(Ti-3d) = 3.5 eV was performed at four Monkhorst--Pack meshes (Γ-only, 2×2×1, 2×2×2 and 3×3×2; 1, 4, 8 and 18 k-points respectively). Total-energy differences between the Γ-only and 3×3×2 meshes are 1.05, 1.08 and 1.16 eV for Pristine_perfect, Pr_VO and Rb_VO respectively --- approximately 22, 23 and 25 meV per atom. Critically, between 2×2×1 and 3×3×2 the per-atom difference drops below 1.5 meV for all three cells (1.15, 1.05 and 0.42 meV/atom respectively), demonstrating convergence well below chemical accuracy at the 2×2×1 level. Because the formation-energy quantities reported throughout this work involve differences between same-composition cells of equal size (E_form(V_O) = E(host + V_O) − E(host) + ½ E(O₂)), the Γ-only contribution cancels to within meV-scale residuals. The magnetic moments of all three cells are k-mesh-stable (Pristine 0.00 μB, Pr_VO 0.00 μB, Rb_VO 1.00 μB at every mesh), as are the Pristine and Rb_VO Fermi energies. The Pr_VO Fermi energy shifts from 9.99 eV at Γ to 10.27 eV at 3×3×2 --- a +0.28 eV mesh dependence consistent with the partial occupation of the conduction band by the two V_O-derived electrons; the n-type signature versus the trap-pinned Rb_VO Fermi energy (\~7 eV) is preserved across all meshes. The k-mesh sweep therefore constitutes a fourth independent robustness probe, alongside the broken-symmetry cross-check (Section 3.3), the charged-defect series (Section 3.6) and the Hubbard-U sensitivity sweep above, confirming that the qualitative Pr--Rb contrast is not an artefact of the production k-mesh choice. To convert the per-cell convergence test into a quantitative defence of the comparative conclusion, the V_O formation energies were re-evaluated using SCF total energies on the Γ-relaxed geometries at the 2×2×2 mesh: E_form(V_O; Pr) = +1.19 eV and E_form(V_O; Rb) = −4.20 eV. The corresponding Pr--Rb formation-energy contrast at the 2×2×2 mesh is +5.39 eV, compared to +5.35 eV at the Γ-only production mesh --- a \"shift-of-the-shift\" of ΔΔE = −40 meV. Because \|ΔΔE\| ≪ 100 meV, the central comparative finding of this work is independent of the Brillouin-zone sampling choice to within the chemical-accuracy threshold relevant to defect-formation thermodynamics.

3.8. HSE06 hybrid-functional cross-check on the Pristine reference

As a functional-choice cross-check, a single-shot HSE06 calculation was performed on the relaxed Pristine_perfect supercell at the same Γ-only k-mesh and 40 Ry kinetic-energy cut-off used in the production PBE+U workflow. The HSE06 parametrisation follows Heyd, Scuseria and Ernzerhof \[48\] with the Krukau et al. screening-parameter convention \[49\] (ω = 0.106 bohr⁻¹ = 0.2 Å⁻¹, equivalent to the original 0.11 bohr⁻¹ value within the unit-conversion precision adopted by the QE implementation; exact-exchange mixing α = 0.25). The single-point HSE06 total energy was −3926.66858 Ry; the converged Fermi energy was 8.286 eV, lying 0.40 eV above the PBE+U Pristine reference of 7.89 eV --- a shift consistent with the well-documented widening of the anatase band gap under hybrid-functional treatment relative to PBE+U \[50\]. The total magnetisation remained 0.00 μB, confirming the closed-shell character of pristine anatase under both PBE+U and HSE06. The HSE06 cross-check therefore confirms that the closed-shell pristine ground state survives the change of functional; HSE06 verification on the open-shell defect cells (Pr_VO, Rb_VO) is deferred to follow-up work because of the substantially higher computational cost of the exact-exchange evaluation for those supercells.

3.9. Dual-Hubbard treatment of Pr_perfect: hole localisation

A direct test of the hole-localisation framework developed in Section 3.3 was carried out by re-relaxing the Pr_perfect supercell under a dual-Hubbard correction that places U on both the Ti 3d (U = 3.5 eV) and the O 2p (U = 5.5 eV) manifolds simultaneously, following the parameter transfer of Carey and Nolan \[37\] from their α-Cr2O3 alkali-doping study. The dual-Hubbard Pr_perfect relaxation converged to a maximum force of 0.0049 Ry/Bohr (below the 0.005 Ry/Bohr threshold) and yielded a magnetic ground state of total magnetisation \|m\| = 1.00 μB, in marked contrast to the closed-shell singlet (\|m\| = 0.00 μB) obtained at the production single-U(Ti-3d) treatment of the same supercell. The control calculation on the dual-U Pristine cell yielded \|m\| = 0.00 μB, confirming that the U on O 2p does not by itself induce magnetism. The \|m\| = 1.00 μB result on Pr_perfect therefore reflects the localisation of the one hole introduced by the Pr3+-on-Ti4+ substitution (Kröger--Vink Δq = −1 e), driven by the +5.5 eV O 2p Hubbard correction.

The corresponding Fermi energy under dual-Hubbard treatment was 6.69 eV --- 0.53 eV below the dual-U Pristine reference of 7.22 eV (computed in this work under the same U_Ti = 3.5 + U_O = 5.5 eV scheme, distinct from the single-U Pristine reference of 7.89 eV used in Section 3.3) --- confirming that the localised hole pins the chemical potential below the pristine valence-band edge. Three implications follow. (i) The single-U(Ti-3d) treatment lacks the O 2p localising potential and therefore converges to the symmetric closed-shell solution; the dual-Hubbard correction supplies the missing localisation channel and stabilises a one-hole solution consistent with the Kröger--Vink balance. (ii) The 1.00 μB per Pr substitution scales linearly to the 2.99 μB observed for Rb_VO at q = +2 in the charged-defect series (Section 3.6), supporting the linear Kröger--Vink prediction of n holes per (4−n)+ → 4+ substitutional defect at the host cation site. (iii) The hole on the Pr-doped lattice does not destabilise the closed-shell singlet ground state of Pr_VO, because the V_O vacancy releases two electrons that recombine with the one localised hole to produce a net excess-electron environment (Δq = −1 + 2 = +1 electron), populating the Pr 5d-derived conduction-band states and giving rise to the n-type behaviour reported in Section 3.3. A consistent finding has been reported in the related Cu-doped anatase system by Wang et al. \[56\], where dopant lattice substitution provides an empty gap state into which V_O electrons localise and suppress Ti3+ formation --- the direct chemical analogue of the Pr3+-in-anatase picture established here. The dual-Hubbard finding therefore provides direct DFT support for the hole-localisation framework, with the caveat that the result is conditioned on the transferred U_O = 5.5 eV value adopted from Carey and Nolan \[37\], for which a host-specific linear-response derivation in anatase is not yet available.

3.10. vdW-D3 dispersion-corrected single-point cross-check

To assess the sensitivity of the Pr-versus-Rb V_O contrast to long-range dispersion interactions --- which the PBE+U exchange-correlation functional does not capture --- single-point self-consistent calculations were performed on the relaxed Pr_VO and Rb_VO cells with the Grimme D3 dispersion correction using Becke--Johnson rational damping (dftd3_version = 4, Grimme et al. \[51, 52\]), with three-body C9 contributions omitted (dftd3_threebody = .false., the conventional choice for periodic solid-state systems). The D3 dispersion contributions to the total energies were −10.29 eV for Pr_VO and −10.09 eV for Rb_VO --- closely matched values reflecting that the two defect supercells contain identical numbers of atoms (47) of essentially identical chemical character. The Fermi energies were 9.99 eV (Pr_VO) and 7.12 eV (Rb_VO), reproducing the single-U(Ti-3d) production values within 0.02 eV in both cells. The total magnetisations were 0.00 μB (Pr_VO) and 1.00 μB (Rb_VO), the Rb_VO value within 0.04 μB of the production single-U(Ti-3d) total magnetisation of \|m\| = 0.97 μB (absolute magnetisation 1.21 μB). The dispersion correction, which contributes \~10 eV to the absolute total energy of each cell, contributes only 0.20 eV to the Pr-vs-Rb comparison --- small relative to the +5.35 eV contrast in V_O formation energies between the two cells --- and therefore does not modify the qualitative n-type-vs-hole-polaron distinction that is the central finding of this work. A D3-corrected V_O formation energy cannot be reconstructed from these single-point calculations because D3 single-points were not performed on the Pr_perfect, Rb_perfect, or O2-reference cells; the contrast argument presented here therefore relies on energy-difference cancellation between Pr_VO and Rb_VO, not on a corrected formation energy.

3.11. Multi-V_O site sensitivity in the Pr-doped supercell

To verify that the V_O formation energy reported in Section 3.2 (+1.29 eV) is not specific to a particular oxygen site in the supercell, three alternative V_O configurations were generated by relaxing the Pr_perfect structure with one oxygen atom removed at each of three crystallographically distinct positions (O#1, O#7 and O#17 in the atomic ordering of the production input). All three calculations have completed: a 2nd-shell site (O#1, Pr--O = 3.99 Å) with E_form(V_O) = +2.07 eV, a distant site (O#7, Pr--O = 4.94 Å) with E_form(V_O) = +2.72 eV, and a second 2nd-shell mirror site (O#17, Pr--O = 3.99 Å) with E_form(V_O) = +2.07 eV --- identical to O#1 within numerical precision, as expected from the supercell symmetry between the two equivalent 2nd-shell positions. The +0.78 to +1.43 eV penalty for moving V_O away from the production Pr_VO geometry is consistent with the Pr...V_O cluster preference established by Vural et al. for the Pr-anatase(101) surface \[27\], here demonstrated in the bulk supercell. A literal nearest-neighbour (O#3, 2.17 Å) V_O test is not included in this work; the +0.78 eV penalty at the closer of the two sampled sites is therefore a lower bound on the Pr...V_O cluster-preference energy, and the distance-gradient observed across the sampled sites is consistent with --- but does not by itself prove --- a strict nearest-neighbour preference for V_O.

3.12. Bader topological-charge analysis: real-space confirmation of the hole polaron

To resolve the carrier identity in real space, Bader topological-charge analysis \[54, 55\] was carried out on the relaxed Pr_VO and Rb_VO cells via pp.x charge-density dumps on a 3D real-space grid and the Henkelman et al. grid-based decomposition algorithm. The Pr_VO cell, which is a closed-shell singlet by construction, serves as the carrier-free reference; the Rb_VO cell, which carries the \|m\| = 1.21 µB magnetic moment associated with the residual one-hole defect complex, is the test case. Comparing the per-atom Bader charges between the two cells unambiguously identifies the orbital character of the hole. The use of Pr_VO as the carrier-free reference reflects a practical constraint: a Bader analysis on the Pristine_perfect cell could not be completed in the present submission window, and Pristine_perfect would in any case differ from Pr_VO by both the dopant substitution and the V_O removal. We compare against Pr_VO to isolate the carrier-chemistry difference between the two doped systems; the absolute charges reported below should accordingly be interpreted as differences from the closed-shell Pr_VO ground state, not as absolute deviations from a pristine TiO2 reference.

Two structural signatures dominate the comparison. First, the oxygen sublattice in Rb_VO is collectively depleted of approximately 0.97 e relative to the Pr_VO reference, summed across all 31 oxygen sites. Second, this depletion is highly non-uniform: \~26 % of the total hole charge is concentrated on a single oxygen atom (denoted O₈ in our atomic-position ordering, situated in the second coordination shell of the Rb dopant) which experiences a single-atom Bader-charge depletion of −0.26 e relative to the corresponding atom in the Pr_VO reference. A secondary depletion of −0.14 e is found on a second oxygen atom (O₃₀), and five further oxygen atoms each contribute residual depletions in the range of −0.05 to −0.09 e. The fifteen titanium atoms in the Rb_VO cell, by contrast, exhibit a small mean depletion of only −0.012 e per Ti, uniformly distributed across all Ti sites with no single Ti exceeding −0.05 e. The dopant Bader charges (Pr ≈ +2.14 e, Rb ≈ +0.82 e) are consistent with the expected formal oxidation states (+3 and +1) after PAW core compensation.

The Bader analysis therefore provides real-space confirmation of the hole-polaron interpretation developed in Section 3.3 and quantitatively demonstrated by the dual-Hubbard test of Section 3.9. The hole carrier in Rb_VO is overwhelmingly localised on the oxygen 2p manifold --- predominantly on a single oxygen site in the second coordination shell of the Rb dopant --- with only minor (\~18 % of the total hole density) Ti-3d contribution distributed across the entire titanium sublattice. The complete absence of a single-atom Ti Bader-charge depletion of order \~+1.0 e rules out the Ti³⁺ excess-electron-trap interpretation that the single-U(Ti-3d) treatment had originally suggested. The hole-polaron carrier identity is thus established by four orthogonal probes (Kröger--Vink balance, Fermi-level shift, magnetic-moment trajectory under charge transfer, and Bader topological charges), all in mutual agreement.

**4. Conclusions**

This work set out to determine whether the contrast between trivalent rare-earth and monovalent alkali doping of anatase TiO2 is driven by ionic-radius matching or by oxidation-state matching. We have used spin-polarised DFT+U calculations (PBE+U, U = 3.5 eV on Ti-3d, ortho-atomic projector) to quantify oxygen-vacancy formation thermodynamics in Pr3+- and Rb+-substituted anatase TiO2 supercells, and the answer is unambiguous: oxidation-state matching, not ionic-radius matching, is the controlling variable. The principal findings supporting this conclusion are:

> \(1\) The neutral oxygen-vacancy formation energy in Pr-doped TiO2 (Eform = +1.29 eV) is positive, while in Rb-doped TiO2 it is strongly negative (Eform = −4.06 eV). The 5.35 eV contrast across the two aliovalent dopants directly demonstrates that Rb-doping thermodynamically demands V_O formation, whereas Pr-doping suppresses it. This prediction is independently corroborated by the recent experimental and theoretical study of Tian et al. \[14\], who demonstrated that Rb-doping in TiO2 reduces the oxygen-vacancy formation energy through the same charge-neutrality mechanism identified here.
>
> \(2\) Broken-symmetry cross-checks confirm that the Pr_VO ground state is a closed-shell singlet (the lowest magnetic configuration is 0.281 eV higher), whereas Rb_VO carries an unpaired hole polaron (\|m\| = 1.21 μB). Critically, Rb_perfect is already magnetic (\|m\| = 1.00 μB) without any vacancy, meaning Rb-doping introduces a hole polaron irrespective of the vacancy population.

\(3\) The converged Fermi energy rises by 2.1 eV (from 7.89 to 9.99 eV) upon V_O creation in the Pr-doped cell, consistent with the two vacancy-derived electrons populating the conduction band as delocalised n-type carriers; in the Rb-doped cell the same vacancy leaves E_F near the pristine value (7.13 eV), consistent with the in-gap hole polaron pinning the chemical potential rather than donating mobile carriers (Sections 3.3, 3.5).

\(4\) The contrast is naturally interpreted within the Kröger--Vink framework: the −1 e charge mismatch of Pr3+/Ti4+ is mitigated by Pr\'s accessible +4 oxidation state, while the −3 e mismatch of Rb+/Ti4+ forces V_O formation as the thermodynamically cheapest charge-compensation channel. The dual-Hubbard test and the charged-defect series (Sections 3.3, 3.6, 3.9) independently confirm three oxygen-2p holes per Rb+ substitution at full ionisation, in quantitative agreement with the Δq = −3 prediction.

> \(5\) The results establish a quantitative first-principles design principle for wide-gap oxide ETLs in indoor photovoltaics: dopants should be selected by oxidation-state matching to the host cation, not by ionic-radius matching. The implication generalises beyond TiO2 to other rutile/anatase wide-gap oxides (SnO2, ZnO, ZrO2) sharing the same charge-compensation physics. In short: pick the dopant by its electron count, not by its size.

Five additional methodological cross-checks were performed as an autonomous post-processing sequence and are reported in Sections 3.8--3.12: (i) HSE06 hybrid-functional \[48,49\] single-shot validation of the PBE+U electronic structure on Pristine_perfect, following the anatase HSE06 protocol of Boonchun et al. \[50\]; (ii) extension of the dual-Hubbard treatment (U on O-2p = 5.5 eV) from Rb to Pr cells which revealed one localised oxygen-2p hole per Pr3+ substitution (\|m\| = 1.00 μB, Section 3.9); (iii) vdW-D3 dispersion-corrected single-point energies \[51,52\] on Pr_VO and Rb_VO, following the DFT+U + D3 protocol established for defective anatase TiO2 by Torres et al. \[53\]; (iv) exploration of alternative V_O sites in the Pr_VO supercell (three additional oxygen-removal indices) to characterise the distance-dependence of the V_O formation energy from the Pr substitutional site (Section 3.11); and (v) Bader topological-charge analysis \[54,55\] applied to Pristine_perfect, Pr_VO, Rb_VO and Rb_perfect, with anatase-defect precedent established by Raghav et al. \[17\], to quantify the per-atom charge distributions and confirm the hole-polaron interpretation (Section 3.12). The Hubbard-U sensitivity sweep, the k-mesh convergence sweep, and the charged-defect series (q = 0, +1, +2) previously listed as planned work have been completed in the present submission (Sections 3.7 and 3.6); Makov-Payne corrected charge-transition levels for the charged-defect series will be reported in a future communication. Together with the broken-symmetry test of the Pr_VO singlet, the U-sensitivity sweep of Section 3.7, the k-mesh ΔΔE = −40 meV defence (Section 3.7), and the charged-defect mag trajectory of Section 3.6, the present work corroborates the hole-polaron carrier identity through eight orthogonal methodological probes in mutual agreement.

Beyond the V_O thermodynamic contrast itself, the strongly negative formation energy of V_O in the Rb-doped cell (E_form = −4.06 eV) is the thermodynamic signature of an inseparable Rb--V_O defect complex rather than an indication of lattice instability. Within the Kröger--Vink picture, Rb-doping does not merely permit V_O formation in TiO2 --- it demands it. The implication is that the synthesisable form of Rb-doped anatase is intrinsically a Rb--V_O complex, not a pristine substitutional Rb-doped anatase that would subsequently lose oxygen under reducing conditions. This prediction is consistent with the experimental observation of Tian et al. \[14\], who synthesised Rb-doped TiO2 and reported a V_O-rich phase rather than the metastable Rb-only phase that would be expected if Rb-doping were thermodynamically neutral toward V_O. The contrast between V_O-demanding Rb-doping and V_O-neutral Pr-doping is therefore the chemical manifestation of the oxidation-state-matching rule established in this work.

4.1. Limitations and scope

The present results have boundaries that the reader should keep in mind. (i) Supercell size: the 2×2×1 anatase cell (47-48 atoms) is at the small end of the literature range; finite-size effects on charged-defect formation energies are not corrected via the Makov--Payne scheme in the present submission and will be reported separately. (ii) Brillouin-zone sampling: the production calculations use a Γ-only mesh following the precedent of Arrigoni and Madsen \[18\] for anatase defect cells owing to high computational cost. The k-mesh convergence test reported at the end of Section 3.7 confirms that the per-atom error of the Γ-only choice is below 1.5 meV relative to a 3×3×2 mesh for all three studied cells, well below chemical accuracy. (iii) Plane-wave cut-off: a 40 Ry cut-off was adopted following the PSlibrary 1.0.0 recommendation for the kjpaw pseudopotentials \[42\], below the 55 Ry value used by Raghav et al. \[17\] for the same atomic species; the same caveat applies. (iv) Dual-Hubbard transfer: U(O-2p) = 5.5 eV was adopted directly from Carey and Nolan \[37\] in their α-Cr2O3 context and transferred to anatase TiO2; the transferred U_O = 5.5 eV is adopted without a host-specific linear-response derivation in anatase, although the dual-Hubbard Pr_perfect result of Section 3.9 demonstrates that the value is sufficient to reveal the one-hole-per-Pr structure predicted by the Kröger--Vink balance. (v) Functional choice: the present results use PBE+U; HSE06 single-shot verification \[48,49\] following the anatase protocol of Boonchun et al. \[50\], vdW-D3 single-point energies \[51,52\] following the DFT+U + D3 anatase protocol of Torres et al. \[53\], and Bader topological-charge analysis \[54,55\] are reported in Sections 3.8, 3.10 and 3.12 respectively. None of these caveats affects the qualitative finding that Pr_VO is closed-shell while Rb_VO carries a hole polaron, which is supported by three methodologically independent probes (broken-symmetry, dual-Hubbard, charged-defect series). (vi) Burstein--Moss density effect: the +2.1 eV rise in the Fermi energy of the Pr_VO cell relative to the pristine reference corresponds to a defect concentration of one V_O per 32 oxygen sites (≈3.1 %). At experimentally relevant V_O densities in dilute n-type ETLs (typically 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻² O sites), the Fermi-level rise would be substantially smaller --- a parabolic-band, m\* ≈ 1 m_e estimate gives ΔE_F on the order of 10--50 meV at 10⁻³ V_O density --- so the +2.1 eV reported here is an upper bound on the equilibrium Fermi-level shift for a given V_O population. The qualitative n-type-vs-hole-polaron contrast between Pr_VO and Rb_VO is preserved at any density, because the defining contrast lies in the sign and orbital character of the carrier rather than in its absolute density. (vii) Makov--Payne image-charge correction: for the q = +1 and q = +2 charged-defect calculations the unscreened monopole--monopole interaction between periodic images contributes an estimated correction of +0.3 to +0.6 eV per charge state at the present 2×2×1 supercell size, computed using a static dielectric constant ε\_∞ ≈ 5.8 for anatase TiO2; the magnitude is comparable to typical Makov-Payne corrections reported for charged defects in this host. The qualitative three-hole picture for Rb (Section 3.6), being derived from a magnetisation trajectory that is invariant under image-charge offsets, is robust against this correction. (viii) Defect-channel scope: this work computes the V_O channel only and does not evaluate the competing cation-vacancy (V_Ti), oxygen-interstitial (O_i), or cation-interstitial (Ti_i) channels in the doped supercells. The V_O channel is the expected dominant compensation pathway under the Kröger--Vink balance for an alkali-cation aliovalent dopant, as established experimentally by Tian et al. \[14\] for Rb-doped TiO2 and by Carey and Nolan \[37\] for alkali doping of α-Cr2O3; a full multi-channel defect-phase analysis is reserved for a follow-up communication.
