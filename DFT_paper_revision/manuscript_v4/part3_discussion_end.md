
**4. Discussion**

**4.1 The energetic ordering follows the formal electron count.** The three formation energies of Table 2 fall in the order pristine (+4.74 eV) > Pr (+1.29 eV) > Rb (−4.06 eV), which is the order of the formal hole count of the vacancy-free cells (0, 1, 3; Table 1) and also of increasing cation radius (Section 4.3). A neutral vacancy releases two electrons. In the pristine cell both must occupy conduction-band-derived states, and the full cost of oxygen removal is paid. In the Pr cell one of the two can instead fill the single acceptor-like hole introduced by Pr^3+^, and in the Rb cell both can fill two of the three holes introduced by Rb^+^. Transferring an electron from a conduction-band-like state to an oxygen-2p-derived hole near the valence-band edge recovers an energy of the order of the band gap, so reductions of a few eV per transferred electron are expected; the reductions found (at least 3.45 eV for one electron in the Pr cell and 8.81 eV for two in the Rb cell; lower bounds because the pristine reference cell is unrelaxed, Section 2.6) are of that order but not equal per electron. The Kohn–Sham gap of the Rb_VO cell is 2.87 eV and the band separation in the broadened densities of states of Pr_VO about 2.5 eV (Section 3.6), both smaller than either per-electron reduction (3.45 eV for Pr, 4.40 eV per electron for Rb), so electron transfer across the gap does not account for the reductions on its own. The remainder would have to come from other contributions, for example the electrostatic interaction between the vacancy and the dopant, which the dependence of E~f~ on the vacancy site in the Pr cell indicates (Section 3.7), lattice relaxation (the Rb ion lies 1.27 Å off its ideal site in Rb_VO, Section 3.1), band filling and interactions with periodic images; the two-dopant design cannot decompose the formation-energy difference into these contributions.

**4.2 Relation to previous work.** The Rb result parallels, in anatase, the behaviour that Carey and Nolan found for alkali dopants in α-Cr~2~O~3~, where the V~O~ formation energy is strongly reduced and oxygen holes are localised with an O-2p Hubbard term {{Carey2017}}. It also agrees in sign with the experimental vacancy-formation trend reported for Rb-doped TiO~2~ by Tian et al. {{Tian2025}}. Because only one alkali was studied here, the radius dependence found by Carey and Nolan across Li–Rb is not tested. The Pr result is consistent with the trivalent-dopant picture of Iwaszuk and Nolan for rutile {{Iwaszuk2011}}: the vacancy is not spontaneous at the oxygen-rich reference, but its cost is reduced by at least 3.45 eV relative to the pristine host, so vacancy formation near Pr becomes accessible as the oxygen chemical potential is lowered; one vacancy per Pr over-compensates the dopant (Table 1), and the fully compensated 2Pr~Ti~ + V~O~ configuration that corresponds to vacancy compensation of a trivalent dopant was not computed. With E~f~(V~O~; Pr) = 1.29 eV + Δμ~O~, oxygen removal near Pr has a negative formation energy once μ~O~ falls more than 1.29 eV below ½E(O~2~), a threshold that carries the common offset of the PBE O~2~ reference (Section 2.7); whether that lies within the stability field of TiO~2~ against lower oxides was not evaluated (Section 5). For Rb the formation energy is negative already at the oxygen-rich limit and decreases further as μ~O~ is lowered, so a vacancy-free Rb~Ti~ cell is not a stable reference at any oxygen chemical potential at which TiO~2~ exists; whether Rb substitutes on the Ti site at all, rather than occupying interstitial or surface sites or forming a secondary phase, was not evaluated. The spin polarisation of the Pr-induced hole when an oxygen-site U is present parallels the oxygen-hole states reported for trivalent dopants in rutile by Iwaszuk and Nolan {{Iwaszuk2011}}.

**4.3 What the two-dopant comparison does and does not isolate.** Pr^3+^ and Rb^+^ differ in formal charge (−1 versus −3 e relative to Ti^4+^), in ionic radius (0.99 versus 1.52 Å {{Shannon1976}}), in bonding (a 5d-bearing trivalent cation versus a closed-shell alkali) and in accessible oxidation states. Both are larger than Ti^4+^ (0.605 Å), so the comparison does show that a large size mismatch is compatible with either sign of E~f~(V~O~) at the oxygen-rich reference; it does not hold size constant, and no strain or fixed-geometry decomposition was computed, so the contrast cannot be apportioned between electronic and elastic contributions. What can be said is that the sign of the contrast and the size of the reductions are consistent with the electron count; because the ordering pristine > Pr > Rb is also the order of increasing cation radius (0.605, 0.99 and 1.52 Å) and Carey and Nolan found a radius dependence at fixed alkali valence {{Carey2017}}, a size-based explanation of the ordering is not excluded. The relaxed Rb_VO cell shows that an elastic channel exists: the Rb ion lies 1.27 Å off its ideal site, in the space vacated by the adjacent oxygen (Section 3.1), but the energy of this relaxation has not been separated from the electronic contribution, and the corresponding Pr geometry has not been analysed. Isolating the two variables would require a controlled series within the same protocol, for example La^3+^, Y^3+^ and Sc^3+^ (same valence, radii from 1.03 to 0.745 Å {{Shannon1976}}) and K^+^ and Na^+^ (same valence as Rb^+^, smaller radii), together with fixed-geometry single points to separate electronic from relaxation contributions.

**4.4 The Pr-substituted parent is method dependent.** The vacancy-free Pr cell is non-magnetic with U on Ti-3d only and carries one unpaired spin (m = 1.00 μ~B~), expected but not yet shown to reside on oxygen, with U on O-2p (Section 3.4). Both solutions contain the one formal hole; the dual-U result is the one in which the spin count equals the hole count, as in the dual-U Rb cells, but it rests on a U(O-2p) value transferred from Cr~2~O~3~ and on calculations that have not been extended to the vacancy cell or to the formation-energy cycle. Until those are available, the Pr-substituted parent should be described as having one hole whose localisation depends on the treatment of oxygen correlation; it is neither closed-shell nor demonstrably trap-free. For the vacancy-containing Pr cell, the Ti-3d-only treatment places the net carrier, one excess electron, in conduction-band-derived states (Table 1 for the count, Figure 4 for the placement); this conclusion rests on a single smeared non-magnetic solution and one magnetic trial, has not been tested with the oxygen-site term, and concerns a cell with one carrier per 570 Å^3^.

**4.5 Implications for dopant screening in oxide electron-transport layers.** The results are consistent, within this protocol and host, with the formal charge mismatch as a candidate screening variable for how strongly a substitutional acceptor lowers the oxygen-vacancy formation energy: both dopants studied lower it, the trivalent dopant leaving the vacancy uphill at the oxygen-rich limit and the monovalent dopant making it spontaneous there. Extending this to other dopants of the same formal charge, and the three statements that follow, are hypotheses that this work motivates but does not test. (i) Trivalent rare-earth dopants other than Pr (La, Nd, Sm) should behave like Pr in the V~O~ energetics, because they share the −1 e mismatch. (ii) Pentavalent donors (Nb^5+^, Ta^5+^) should raise rather than lower E~f~(V~O~), having a +1 e mismatch, the electronic counterpart of the trivalent case. (iii) The acceptor states introduced by alkali substitution are candidates for recombination centres at an ETL/absorber interface. Testing (iii) requires interface models, charge-transition levels with finite-size corrections and carrier-capture calculations, none of which are reported here, so no ranking of dopants by device performance is made.

**5. Limitations**

(i) *Supercell and concentration.* One dopant and one vacancy per 2×2×1 cell correspond to 6.25 % cation substitution and 3.1 % anion vacancies. Defect–image interactions and band filling by the net carrier of each vacancy cell are not corrected; absolute formation energies at the dilute limit may differ by several tenths of an eV, and because the Pr cell carries a conduction electron and the Rb cell a valence hole these terms need not cancel in ΔE~f~. (ii) *Brillouin-zone sampling.* Production relaxations are Γ-only. Γ-only total energies lie 22–25 meV per atom below the 3×3×2 values (Section 3.7); formation energies rely on cancellation within each host, and the 2×2×2 check shifts each doped-host E~f~ by 0.10–0.14 eV and ΔE~f~ by 0.04 eV, which estimates rather than bounds the residual; the pristine E~f~ was not checked. (iii) *Plane-wave cut-off.* 40 Ry was not tested against a higher cut-off; a comparable anatase study uses 55 Ry {{Raghav2020}}. (iv) *Force tolerance.* 0.005 Ry/Bohr (0.129 eV/Å) is loose; the Pr_VO relaxation was stopped manually and the pristine V~O~ relaxation ended at 0.0056 Ry/Bohr. The production pristine perfect cell was not relaxed (residual force 0.0225 Ry/Bohr, Section 2.6), so the pristine formation energy and the two reductions measured from it are lower bounds, short by its relaxation energy, which was not computed. The effect of tighter relaxation on the energetics and on the localisation results has not been tested. (v) *Spin search.* The production relaxations of the doped cells started from zero magnetisation (the pristine V~O~ cell from 0.05; Section 2.5), and one magnetic trial was performed, for Pr_VO; occupations, smearing and symmetry settings were not varied, and two spin solutions of Rb_VO were found that differ by 6.4 meV (Section 3.3). Because E~f~ is a difference of total energies, a lower-energy spin solution of any parent or vacancy cell, in particular of Pr_perfect, which was not given a magnetic trial, would shift the corresponding E~f~ by the same amount. (vi) *Pr-4f treatment.* The Pr dataset freezes the 4f electrons in the core. Mechanisms involving 4f occupation, 4f hybridisation or a Pr^3+^/Pr^4+^ change of oxidation state cannot be examined with it, and none is claimed; the trivalent state, and with it the formal carrier counts of the Pr cells in Table 1, is imposed by this choice rather than tested. (vii) *Oxygen-site U.* U(O-2p) = 5.5 eV is transferred, not derived for anatase; the dual-U treatment was applied to the vacancy-free cells and Rb_VO (whose relaxation was not completed when logged) but not to Pr_VO or to the formation-energy cycles. (viii) *Charged cells.* No electrostatic or potential-alignment correction was applied; charged-cell energies are not interpreted. An ion-clamped dielectric constant of ε~∞~ ≈ 5.8 (experimental in-plane value, 5.82, as tabulated by Arrigoni and Madsen {{Arrigoni2020}}) would give a first-order monopole correction {{Freysoldt2014}} of a few tenths of an eV for q = +1 at this cell size and four times that for q = +2. The static constant of anatase is much larger and would reduce the estimate, so these numbers are upper bounds and are not used. (ix) *Oxygen reference.* All formation energies are at μ~O~ = ½E(O~2~) (oxygen-rich limit) without finite-temperature corrections; equilibrium vacancy concentrations, dopant incorporation energies, the stability field of TiO~2~ against lower oxides and competing Pr and Rb oxides were not evaluated, and the O~2~ overbinding of PBE was not corrected; no statement about the synthesisable phase of Rb-doped anatase is therefore made. (x) *Defect channels and stoichiometry.* Only a single V~O~ per cell was computed; sequential vacancies, the fully compensated 2 Pr + 1 V~O~ and 2 Rb + 3 V~O~ stoichiometries, cation vacancies, interstitials (including Rb interstitials, a plausible alternative for a large monovalent ion), surface sites and dopant clustering were not. (xi) *Functional.* The HSE06 check concerns the pristine cell only; the doped and defective cells have not been verified with a hybrid functional. (xii) *Dispersion.* The D3 correction to the formation energies is undetermined because the parent-cell terms were not computed. (xiii) *Charge and spin analysis.* The Bader comparison is between cells that differ in composition and by two carriers. The Löwdin moments that place the Rb_VO hole mainly on one oxygen depend on the projector basis and have not been cross-checked with a spin-density isosurface or another partitioning, and the relaxations retained the mirror plane of the defect cell, so hole configurations that break it were not sampled; for Pr_perfect and the dual-U cells no site-resolved analysis exists. (xiv) *Scope.* No surface, interface, transition-level, capture-coefficient or device calculation is reported; statements about electron-transport layers are hypotheses.

**6. Conclusions**

Using one PBE+U protocol in the same 2×2×1 anatase supercell, we find neutral oxygen-vacancy formation energies of +4.74 eV (pristine; a lower bound, because the perfect reference cell was not relaxed), +1.29 eV (Pr-substituted) and −4.06 eV (Rb-substituted) at the oxygen-rich reference. Both substitutions lower the cost of oxygen removal; Pr leaves it uphill and Rb makes it spontaneous. The Pr–Rb contrast of 5.36 eV changes by 0.04 eV between Γ-only and 2×2×2 sampling. The ordering follows the formal electron count: one hole per Pr^3+^ and three per Rb^+^, with a single vacancy over-compensating Pr and leaving one residual hole in the Rb cell. The Rb cells carry unpaired spin in every treatment tested. In Rb_VO the residual hole is an empty minority-spin O-2p band whose Löwdin moment sits mainly on one oxygen bonded to the Rb ion (0.59 of 1.01 μ~B~), and the Rb ion lies 1.27 Å off its ideal site towards the vacancy; a charged-cell series reaches three unpaired spins per Rb^+^. The Pr cells converge to non-magnetic smeared solutions with U on Ti-3d only, but the vacancy-free Pr cell develops an unpaired spin when U is also placed on O-2p, so the spin state of the hole in Pr-substituted anatase is method dependent and a closed-shell, trap-free description of that material is not supported. The two-dopant comparison is consistent with charge-compensation control of oxygen-vacancy energetics in this host and provides common-protocol values for one trivalent and one monovalent dopant, but it does not separate oxidation state from ionic size and bonding, and it does not establish interfacial recombination behaviour. Completing the dual-U formation-energy cycles, tightening the relaxations and adding a matched-valence dopant series are the natural next steps.
