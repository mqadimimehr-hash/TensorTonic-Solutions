**Abstract**

Oxygen vacancies (V~O~) in wide-gap oxide electron-transport layers cause trap-assisted recombination; aliovalent substitution is the usual lever for controlling them, yet trivalent rare-earth and monovalent alkali dopants have rarely been compared under one protocol. We compare Pr^3+^ and Rb^+^ substitution on the Ti site of anatase TiO~2~ with spin-polarised PBE+U calculations (U = 3.5 eV on Ti-3d, 2×2×1 supercell) and a common oxygen reference, μ~O~ = ½E(O~2~). The neutral V~O~ formation energy falls from +4.74 eV in pristine anatase to +1.29 eV in the Pr-substituted cell and −4.06 eV in the Rb-substituted cell; the 5.36 eV Pr–Rb contrast changes by 0.04 eV on a 2×2×2 k-mesh. The ordering follows the formal electron count, one hole per Pr^3+^ and three per Rb^+^, so one vacancy over-compensates Pr and leaves one residual hole with Rb. That hole is an empty minority-spin O-2p state with 0.59 μ~B~ on one oxygen bonded to Rb; the Rb ion itself moves 1.27 Å towards the vacancy, so relaxation also contributes to the Rb result. The Rb cells carry unpaired spin in every treatment tested; the +2-charged vacancy cell reaches a total magnetisation of 2.99 μ~B~. With U on Ti-3d only the Pr cells converge to non-magnetic smeared solutions, but the vacancy-free Pr cell develops one unpaired spin when an O-2p Hubbard term is added, so its spin state is method dependent. The results are consistent with charge-compensation control of V~O~ energetics; the two-dopant design does not separate oxidation state from size and bonding.

**Keywords:** DFT+U; anatase TiO~2~; oxygen vacancy; aliovalent substitution; hole localisation; electron-transport layer.

**1. Introduction**

Indoor photovoltaics are a leading candidate to replace disposable batteries in the growing population of low-power sensors {{Pecunia2021}}. Under the 200–1000 lx illumination typical of indoor spaces the open-circuit voltage is limited by non-radiative recombination **[NEEDS CITATION: typical indoor illuminance and the low-light V~oc~ limitation; Pecunia et al. may serve if the full text supports both]**, and wide-gap absorbers such as CsPbBr~3~ {{Duan2018}} are paired with n-type oxide electron-transport layers (ETLs), for example TiO~2~ or SnO~2~ {{Kavan2020}}. The same oxides serve as electron-transport layers in organic light-emitting diodes {{Nagar2020}}. In TiO~2~ the oxygen vacancy (V~O~) is the dominant native defect: microscopy, photoemission, transient spectroscopy and first-principles work agree that its two excess electrons can either delocalise into the conduction band or localise as Ti^3+^ centres, and that the localised configuration acts as a trap {{Xie2020,Kreft2020,Yin2020,Li2020,Knez2020,Bigi2020,Bai2020,Yin2018,Raghav2020,Arrigoni2020}}. Controlling the equilibrium V~O~ population of an ETL is therefore a materials-design problem, and substitutional aliovalent doping is a widely used lever {{Tian2025,Carey2017,Iwaszuk2011,Han2020}}.

For trivalent dopants on the Ti^4+^ site the formal charge mismatch is −1 e per site. Lanthanide substitution in anatase has been studied with DFT+U for Ce, Pr, Eu and Gd, including Pr together with an oxygen vacancy {{Chen2012}}, for Pr in the bulk {{Chen2014}} and for Er at the (101) surface together with oxygen or titanium vacancies {{Mazierski2020}}; for Ce-doped zirconium titanate both hole formation and oxygen-vacancy formation have been considered as compensation channels, with electron paramagnetic resonance pointing to vacancy compensation {{Polliotto2020}}. **[AUTHOR ACTION: confirm from the full text what Chen et al. (2014) report on the compensation of Pr in anatase.]** The picture is not universal: Iwaszuk and Nolan showed for trivalent dopants in bulk rutile that oxygen-vacancy compensation can compete with, and in some cases dominate, electronic compensation {{Iwaszuk2011}}. The coupling between a lanthanide dopant and an oxygen vacancy has been analysed for Ce-doped anatase {{Albuquerque2014}} and Gd-doped CeO~2~ {{Han2020}}, and Bahmanrokh et al. proposed a defect-equilibria formalism for Ce-doped anatase in which both channels appear {{Bahmanrokh2020}} **[AUTHOR ACTION: confirm from the full text that both electronic and vacancy compensation appear in that formalism.]**. Pr in particular has been studied as an adsorbate on the anatase (101) surface {{Vural2023}} and as a substituent in the bulk {{Chen2012,Chen2014}}, and is an attractive test case, here modelled as trivalent with a 4f-in-core dataset (Section 2.1), because its ionic radius (0.99 Å in six-fold coordination {{Shannon1976}}) is far from that of Ti^4+^ (0.605 Å). For monovalent alkali dopants the mismatch is −3 e per site, and the available evidence points consistently to V~O~ formation as the compensation channel. Carey and Nolan found that Li, Na, K and Rb substitution in α-Cr~2~O~3~ strongly reduces the V~O~ formation energy, with the larger alkalis having the larger effect {{Carey2017}}. Hybrid-functional work on alkali-doped α-Bi~2~O~3~ reached a similar conclusion {{Li2020Bi}}, and Tian et al. recently combined experiment and calculation to show that Rb doping, in synergy with lattice strain, promotes oxygen-vacancy formation in TiO~2~ {{Tian2025}}.

What is missing is a comparison of the two dopant families in the same anatase host with the same supercell, pseudopotentials, Hubbard treatment and oxygen reference; the Rb case of Tian et al., for example, is embedded in a strained photochromic system and is not paired with a trivalent dopant. Without such a comparison, differences between published trivalent and monovalent results cannot be separated from differences in host, protocol or reference state. The comparison is also a useful test of the Kröger–Vink bookkeeping that is routinely invoked for dopant selection {{Kroger1956}}: a Pr^3+^ substitution should leave one hole and a Rb^+^ substitution three, so a single neutral V~O~ (two electrons) should over-compensate Pr and under-compensate Rb.

This work provides that bounded comparison. Using one PBE+U protocol we compute the neutral V~O~ formation energy in pristine, Pr-substituted and Rb-substituted 2×2×1 anatase supercells, characterise the spin state and electron count of each cell, and test the sensitivity of the localisation picture to an oxygen-site Hubbard term. We also report charged-cell spin trends, Bader-charge differences, a hybrid-functional check on the pristine cell, dispersion-corrected single points, a k-mesh verification and alternative vacancy sites. We state explicitly which conclusions the two-dopant design supports: it establishes the ordering and magnitude of the V~O~ energetics within this protocol and shows that they follow the formal charge count, but it does not isolate oxidation state from ionic radius or dopant-specific bonding, and it does not establish device-level recombination behaviour. The bulk formation energies reported here are the reference against which surface and interface V~O~ energetics in ETL films would be measured; interface models are outside the present scope. Section 2 describes the protocol, Section 3 reports structure, energetics, electronic structure and sensitivity tests, Section 4 discusses the results against prior work, Section 5 states the limitations and Section 6 concludes.

**2. Computational Methods**

**2.1 Code, functional and pseudopotentials.** All calculations were performed with Quantum ESPRESSO v7.5 {{Giannozzi2017,Giannozzi2020}} (hardware and wall times in Table S6) using the PBE exchange–correlation functional {{PBE1996}} and projector-augmented-wave (PAW) datasets from PSlibrary 1.0.0 {{PSlibrary}}: Ti.pbe-spn-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0, O.pbe-n-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0, Pr.pbe-spdn-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0 and Rb.pbe-spn-kjpaw_psl.1.0.0. The valence configurations and electron counts of these datasets are listed in Table 1 because they fix the electron count of every supercell. The Pr dataset belongs to the PSlibrary "spdn" lanthanide family, which is generated for the trivalent ion with the 4f electrons frozen in the core; its 11 valence electrons comprise the 5s, 5p, 5d and 6s/6p shells (the generation configuration uses fractional 6s/6p occupations). The Pr-4f states are therefore not variational in the present calculations, and no conclusion about 4f occupation or 4f hybridisation can be drawn from them (Section 5). The Bader electron totals of the Pr~Ti~ + V~O~ and Rb~Ti~ + V~O~ cells (376.998 and 374.998 e, Table S3) reproduce the counts in Table 1 and confirm the valence assignment. Table S7 lists the MD5 checksums printed by pw.x and the atomic wavefunctions that projwfc.x reads from each dataset (Ti 3s/3p/3d, O 2s/2p, Pr 5p/5d, Rb 4s); the Pr dataset contains no 4f wavefunction. **[AUTHOR ACTION: add the MD5 checksum of the Pr dataset and the "Valence configuration" block of each UPF header to Table S7.]** Kinetic-energy cut-offs of 40 Ry (wavefunctions) and 320 Ry (charge density) were used throughout. No cut-off convergence test was performed; comparable anatase defect work uses 55 Ry {{Raghav2020}}, and the consequences are discussed in Section 5. **[AUTHOR ACTION: quote the suggested minimum cut-offs printed in the headers of the four UPF files, and run one 55 Ry single point on Pr_perfect and Pr_VO to bound the cut-off error on E~f~.]**

**2.2 Hubbard correction.** A Hubbard term in the rotationally invariant Dudarev form {{Dudarev1998}}, which is the form implemented behind the HUBBARD card of Quantum ESPRESSO 7.x, was applied to the Ti-3d manifold with U~eff~ = 3.5 eV using ortho-atomic projectors; U~eff~ is written U, U(Ti-3d) or U(O-2p) in the rest of the paper. Deák et al. note that this value is close to an ab initio determination of U for anatase {{Deak2014}}, and it has been used for Er-doped anatase {{Mazierski2020}} and for anatase (101) defect chemistry {{Yin2020}}; other anatase work uses U = 4.2 eV {{Raghav2020}}, a value originally fitted for the rutile (110) surface {{Morgan2007}}, or larger values {{Arrigoni2020}}. Literature U values obtained with other projector schemes are not strictly transferable to the ortho-atomic projectors used here, and the sensitivity of the present results to U is reported in Section 3.7. **[AUTHOR ACTION: verify against the full text that Deák et al. quote an ab initio anatase value near 3.5 eV (the values of Mazierski et al. and Yin et al. were checked against their texts).]** The DFT+U approach rests on the formulation of Anisimov et al. {{Anisimov1991}}; linear-response determination of U {{Cococcioni2005}} was not performed for any cell in this work. For the oxygen-site tests of Section 3.4 an additional U~eff~ = 5.5 eV was placed on the O-2p manifold. This value is transferred from the Cr~2~O~3~ study of Carey and Nolan {{Carey2017}}, where it was used together with a Cr-3d term to localise oxygen holes; it has not been derived for anatase and is treated here as a sensitivity parameter rather than a calibrated value.

**2.3 Supercells.** A 2×2×1 anatase supercell with a = b = 7.63632 Å and c = 9.77900 Å (volume 570.25 Å^3^) was used, containing 48 atoms (Ti~16~O~32~) in the perfect cell and 47 after removal of one oxygen atom. Dopants were introduced by replacing one Ti atom with Pr or Rb, i.e. 6.25 % of cation sites; one V~O~ per cell corresponds to 3.1 % of oxygen sites or 1.75×10^21^ cm^−3^. These concentrations are far above the dilute limit relevant to ETLs, and all absolute quantities below should be read as those of a concentrated periodic defect array. Lattice parameters were taken from a variable-cell relaxation of the pristine cell and held fixed for all defect cells. **[AUTHOR ACTION: state the k-mesh, cut-off and residual stress of that variable-cell relaxation.]** The production V~O~ is a first-shell oxygen of the dopant site: in the relaxed Rb_VO coordinates the vacant position is an equatorial oxygen site 1.95 Å from the ideal cation position (Section 3.1, Table S8), and the two doped cells have identical atom ordering in their outputs, consistent with the same vacancy. Three alternative sites in the Pr cell are examined in Section 3.7; a second first-shell candidate (index O3 of the orchestration script) was planned but not computed. **[AUTHOR ACTION: confirm from the Pr_VO input that the same oxygen was removed, and give the relaxed first-shell Pr–O distances in Table S8; the value ≈2.17 Å quoted for the production site in earlier drafts is well below the Shannon-radius sum of 2.39 Å for six-fold Pr^3+^–O^2−^.]**

**2.4 Brillouin-zone sampling and electronic settings.** Production relaxations used Γ-point sampling; in earlier anatase defect work a Γ-only grid was used only for the hybrid-functional calculations, with denser meshes otherwise {{Arrigoni2020}}. Single-point total energies on the Γ-relaxed geometries were recomputed on a 2×2×2 Monkhorst–Pack mesh for all four doped cells, and the projected densities of states and Löwdin moments were obtained from non-self-consistent calculations on a 2×2×2 mesh (Figure 4; Supporting Information, Section S2); the k-mesh dependence of the formation-energy contrast is reported in Section 3.7. All calculations were spin-polarised (nspin = 2). Fractional occupations were treated with Gaussian smearing. The single-point inputs derived from the production runs (U sweep, Section 3.7) use degauss = 0.005 Ry, whereas the converged pristine-V~O~ relaxation (Section S1) and the self-consistent runs that regenerated the densities for the projected densities of states used 0.01 Ry (Table S10). **[AUTHOR ACTION: confirm degauss = 0.005 Ry in the production relaxation inputs.]** With smearing, the total energy printed by pw.x is the smearing free energy; the entropy term is small when few states lie near the Fermi level but has not been tabulated. **[AUTHOR ACTION: report the "smearing contrib. (−TS)" line for every cell in Table S10; if it exceeds 0.01 Ry for any defect cell, report internal energies as well.]** The electronic self-consistency threshold was 1×10^−8^ Ry. Symmetry was handled by the code's default detection. **[AUTHOR ACTION: state whether nosym or any magnetisation constraint (tot_magnetization, constrained_magnetization) was used in any run.]**

**2.5 Magnetisation conventions and spin initialisation.** Two quantities are reported throughout: the total (net) magnetisation m = ∫[ρ~↑~(r) − ρ~↓~(r)] dr, which can be of either sign, and the absolute magnetisation m~abs~ = ∫|ρ~↑~(r) − ρ~↓~(r)| dr. For a single unpaired electron, localised or delocalised, both are close to 1 μ~B~, so neither quantity measures localisation; m~abs~ > |m| indicates spin density of both signs. The run log records starting_magnetization = 0 on every species for the production relaxations, and the single-point inputs derived from them use the same setting; pw.x accepts explicitly set zero values with nspin = 2 and stops only when starting_magnetization is not set for any species. From a spin-symmetric start a moment develops only if the symmetric solution is unstable and numerical noise (here the randomised starting wavefunctions) breaks the symmetry, as happened for both Rb cells; the same start can leave a calculation in a non-magnetic solution when a magnetic one exists. **[AUTHOR ACTION: confirm the starting_magnetization values from the production input files.]** For this reason the Pr_VO cell was re-relaxed from a magnetic start with starting_magnetization = 0.3 on Ti and 0.5 on the dopant under a separate prefix, and the two total energies were compared (Section 3.3). These starting values are fractions of the valence charge in the Quantum ESPRESSO convention, not site moments in μ~B~. The converged pristine-V~O~ relaxation started from 0.05 on Ti and O and ended non-magnetic (Section S1), and the self-consistent run preceding the Rb_VO projected densities of states started from 0.3 on Ti and reproduced the production Fermi energy (7.1329 eV). No magnetic start was tried for Pristine_perfect or, with U on Ti-3d only, for Pr_perfect. A wider search over site-specific and antiparallel seeds was not performed, and the non-magnetic solutions are therefore described below as the lowest-energy solutions among those tested rather than as established ground states.

**2.6 Geometry optimisation.** Ionic positions were relaxed with BFGS until the largest force component fell below 0.005 Ry/Bohr (0.129 eV/Å). For the doped cells, charge mixing was damped (mixing_beta = 0.1) and the BFGS history shortened (bfgs_ndim = 3) to suppress oscillation about shallow minima; the pristine V~O~ cell was first attempted with mixing_beta = 0.3 (mixing_mode = local-TF) and converged only after the changes described in Section S1. The final maximum forces were 0.0026, 0.0042, 0.0037 and 0.0040 Ry/Bohr (0.067, 0.108, 0.095 and 0.103 eV/Å) for Pr_perfect, Pr_VO, Rb_perfect and Rb_VO. The run log lists an earlier pristine perfect run as a single-point calculation with a residual force of 0.0225 Ry/Bohr (0.58 eV/Å); if the production pristine energy is also unrelaxed, relaxing it would lower E(Pristine_perfect) and raise the pristine formation energy of Table 2 by the relaxation energy. **[AUTHOR ACTION: state whether the production Pristine_perfect energy (−4275.01530 Ry) comes from a relaxed geometry and give its maximum residual force; if not, relax it and update Table 2.]** The Pr_VO relaxation was stopped manually after the force fell below the threshold while the BFGS step oscillated; the energy change over the last iterations was below 1×10^−9^ Ry. **[AUTHOR ACTION: state etot_conv_thr and forc_conv_thr of the production inputs; with the maximum force below 0.005 Ry/Bohr for several steps (Figure S3) and an energy change below 1×10^−9^ Ry, pw.x would normally have terminated by itself unless a tighter threshold was set.]** This tolerance is looser than the 0.01–0.03 eV/Å commonly used for defect energetics, and its consequences are discussed in Section 5. **2.7 Formation energies.** The neutral V~O~ formation energy of host X was evaluated as

E~f~(V~O~^0^; X) = E(X with V~O~, q = 0) − E(X perfect, q = 0) + μ~O~, with μ~O~ = ½E(O~2~) + Δμ~O~ and Δμ~O~ = 0,

where E(O~2~) is the total energy of an isolated O~2~ molecule in a 12 Å cubic box computed with the same settings; it converged to the triplet ground state (m = 2.00 μ~B~) and ½E(O~2~) = −41.51621 Ry (−564.857 eV). This is the oxygen-rich electronic-energy reference; no finite-temperature gas-phase correction or competing-phase analysis was applied, and the values are conditional oxygen-removal energies from the already substituted cells rather than equilibrium vacancy concentrations {{Freysoldt2014}}. The known overbinding of O~2~ by PBE {{Arrigoni2020}} shifts all absolute formation energies at this reference by a common amount and cancels in differences between hosts; no empirical correction was applied. Formation energies were evaluated from unrounded Rydberg totals and are reported to 0.01 eV; the full-precision ledger is Table S9. The comparative quantity ΔE~f~ = E~f~(V~O~; Pr) − E~f~(V~O~; Rb) is independent of the oxygen reference.
